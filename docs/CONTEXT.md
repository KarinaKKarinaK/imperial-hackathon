## Hackathon rules (from the Imperial challenge brief)

### Wild Card track: what it is
- For real, urgent, underexplored problems grounded in the Hong Kong or Macau Policy Address that none of Challenges 1 to 5 cover.
- It is judged as a harder track, not an easier one. Teams are scored on quality of problem identification as well as the solution.
- Capped at five university teams. Places go on quality of the problem statement.
- Mentors will challenge our assumptions, not validate them.

### The one rule that cannot be broken
Every submission must be explicitly anchored in at least one policy priority from the Hong Kong or Macau Policy Address that is not the primary focus of Challenges 1 to 5. Name the priority, quote the document directly, and explain why the existing challenges do not cover it.

### What Challenges 1 to 5 already cover (so we must stay clear)
1. Smart ageing and gerontechnology
2. Responsible AI and public service governance
3. Green technology and decarbonisation
4. Traditional Chinese medicine and biomedical convergence
5. Smart infrastructure and GBA connectivity (eVTOL, drones, maritime logistics, microgrids)

Our framing: occupational health and injury compensation for platform workers. Not AI governance, not infrastructure. Do not drift into drones or public service AI.

### What counts as a valid open challenge problem
- Real: grounded in documented, verifiable evidence affecting people in HK or Macau now.
- Genuinely uncovered by Challenges 1 to 5.
- Solvable within the sprint: a credible, evidence-based prototyped response, not a complete solution.
- Requires genuine STEMB convergence: cannot be solved by one discipline alone.

### Suggested workflow from Imperial (this is effectively the marking scheme)

Step 1, Evidence base (all pillars)
- Structured review, minimum ten peer-reviewed sources, using a recognised evidence synthesis framework.
- Quantify scale and burden with epidemiological, economic or engineering metrics.
- Name the specific gap and explain why it has persisted.

Step 2, Design and develop the solution (engineering and natural sciences)
- Research-grade prototype, model, system or tool grounded in peer-reviewed methodology.
- Quantitative or computational methods, documented so someone else could replicate it.
- Failure mode or limitation analysis: when would it not work and how would we know.

Step 3, Validate and stress-test (natural sciences and engineering)
- Validate against real or simulated data.
- Sensitivity or scenario analysis.
- Report uncertainty: confidence intervals, error bounds or scenario ranges.
- Be explicit about what results do and do not prove.

Step 4, Translation and implementation strategy (business)
- Regulatory or approval pathway.
- Financial model: development cost, funding sources, return on investment or social return.
- Key stakeholders in government, industry and civil society.
- Risk register with the top three barriers and how to address them.

Step 5, Ethics, governance, research integrity (all pillars)
- Ethical implications for the affected population.
- Data governance framework.
- Conflicts of interest and dual-use risks.
- Limits of the evidence base and what further research is needed before deployment at scale.

### STEMB integration map (mandatory, assessed standalone)
One page: a diagram plus a 50-word narrative showing how each of the four pillars contributed to problem identification, solution design, validation and translation. It is assessed as a standalone component of the scientific poster. Weak integration scores badly regardless of technical quality.

Expected pillar roles:

- Natural sciences: evidence base, validation, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty quantification.
- Medicine and health: human health or social welfare dimension, ethical implications, clinical or community relevance.
- Business: financial model, regulatory pathway, stakeholder strategy, risk register.

## Our idea

### Problem statement (as submitted, 2 Oct 2026, approved by Imperial)

> 2026 Policy Address Para 385 commits to legislation giving delivery platform workers "statutory compensation for injuries at work". Platforms will bear this liability, yet no objective data verifies work-time incidents or prices risk; a 2024 union survey found only 13% of injured riders compensated. This occupational-health gap lies outside Challenges 1-5. Can low-cost helmet and phone telemetry with ML detect crashes and predict fatigue and heat risk? We will prototype it, validated via drop tests, enabling trauma triage and parametric micro-insurance.

Earlier anchor (2025 Policy Address, para 256, now superseded but useful as background):

> 256. The prevalence of digital platforms, such as those providing food and goods delivery services, has brought convenience to the public and created job opportunities. The Government will improve the work injury compensation mechanism for digital platform workers through legislation, and continue to make good use of the Tripartite Committee for the Digital Platform Industry to discuss issues of concern to the stakeholders.

### The problem in plain words
- Delivery riders in Hong Kong (Foodpanda, Deliveroo, KeeTa) are independent contractors, so the Employees' Compensation Ordinance does not cover them. No sick leave, no guaranteed medical cover, no injury compensation.
- Platform algorithms push for speed. Tight windows and late penalties push riders to run red lights and work through heat and storms.
- The government has now promised statutory compensation (2026 Policy Address, para 385). Platforms will carry the liability.
- But nobody has objective data on when and how riders get hurt. Insurers cannot price the risk. Platforms cannot verify a claim happened on a job. Riders cannot prove it. In a 2021 union survey (HKFTU) only 2 of 22 injured riders were compensated by the platform; the earlier 13% figure was dropped as untraceable. Original note: a 2024 union survey found only 13% of injured riders were compensated.
- Result: a deadlock. The legislation needs data that does not exist yet.

### The solution
Low-cost telemetry (a helmet sensor plus the rider's phone) with ML that:
1. Detects crashes with a gradient boosting model on ten window features, trained on the UMAFall public dataset and synthetic rider scenarios (ml/out/results.md). The old three-threshold rule is kept as the baseline row.
2. Flags fatigue (long shift hours today; speed and steering variance planned).
3. Scores heat risk (Hong Kong Observatory heat index plus exertion signals).

What the data drives:
- Trauma triage: on a detected crash, send location and a probable trauma profile to a dispatcher.
- Parametric micro-insurance: a verified work-time crash event is the trigger. Payout is automatic, no claims fight. Premium per delivery window, billed to the platform, adjusted by weather and risk.
- Objective incident record: the evidence base the new legislation needs.

### Why this is cross-disciplinary (STEMB)
- Engineering: helmet hardware, phone app, data pipeline, ML detection.
- Natural sciences: drop test design, sensor calibration, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty.
- Medicine and health: injury mechanisms, triage profiles, heat stress and fatigue thresholds, worker wellbeing.
- Business: parametric insurance design, Hong Kong Insurance Authority pathway, platform economics, risk register.

## Business framing (updated 17 Oct 2026, full numbers in research/MARKET_REFRAMING.md)

### The honest market picture
- Hong Kong has about 12,900 delivery platform workers (official survey, Dec 2023 to Mar 2024). Two platforms left after Deliveroo exited in 2025. A rider-only Hong Kong product is a pilot, not a market.
- Macau: low thousands of riders, no official count, government says no rules planned. A mirror case, not a market.
- Mainland China: 84 million "new forms of employment" workers. National occupational injury scheme for platform workers went nationwide in Jul 2026 across 14 platforms. Meituan alone has 10 to 13 million riders and has paid over 3bn yuan in contributions. Premiums are flat, claims are manual.
- Singapore made platform worker injury insurance mandatory on 1 Jan 2025.
- Domestic helpers are out of scope. They are employees with compulsory compensation insurance already.

### What we are
A verified, signed work-injury event record for every delivery worker, on foot, bicycle or motorbike. The phone they already carry is the sensor; the 3D-printed helmet clip is the upgrade for motorbike riders. The record names the app and the order active at impact and the seconds left on the delivery clock, so apportionment is a lookup and time pressure becomes measurable. Built in Hong Kong for the riders para 385 covers, priced through parametric insurance, designed as a standard that travels. We are the sensor, the trigger and the data standard. We are not the insurer.

### Why the business case holds
1. The Jul 2026 Hong Kong proposal makes platforms liable on a no-fault basis. They will insure it. Insurers must price a risk with no history.
2. The same proposal apportions liability between platforms when a rider is on several apps at the moment of injury. One third of HK riders are. Nobody can settle that split without a record of which job was active at impact. Lead the pitch with this.
3. Who pays: platforms and insurers, never riders. Monthly fee per insured rider plus a per-claim verification fee.
4. Hong Kong pilot is worth low single-digit millions of HK dollars a year. Say so. The scale column is one large platform on the mainland or in Southeast Asia.

### Judge questions and answers
- "Hong Kong is tiny." Yes. It is the first common-law jurisdiction legislating a no-fault platform worker regime with multi-platform apportionment. Right place to set the standard, not to make money.
- "Why would a platform adopt something that proves riders got hurt?" They are liable either way. Verified data lowers premiums, settles apportionment and defends against misattributed claims. The Foodpanda typhoon case cost HK$1.35m and five years.
- "Why would riders wear it?" Faster payout, no claims fight, a record that works for them. Consent and data ownership rules make it safe.
- "Why would a mainland platform buy from you?" They may not. The standard travels through insurers and reinsurers working across the border. It is in the risk register.

## Background research collected so far

### Gig economy regulation elsewhere (for the "why now" and comparison slide)
- EU Platform Work Directive (2024/2831), in force Dec 2024, transposition deadline 2 Dec 2026. Presumption of employment when the platform exerts control. Human oversight required for account suspensions. Bans automated processing of workers' emotional or psychological state. Relevant to us: our fatigue signal must not become a psychological profiling tool. Design it as a safety alert to the rider, not a score the platform sees.
- US federal: DOL 2024 six-factor test, proposed rescission Feb 2026 toward a two-factor test.
- California Prop 22: contractors with a wage floor (120% of minimum for engaged time), per-mile reimbursement (USD 0.37 in 2026), healthcare stipend. A hybrid model similar to where Hong Kong is heading.

### Rejected alternative ideas (do not revive)
Construction site robot allocation ("SiteSense HK"), apprenticeship placement matching, working-time limits, hospital logistics robots, AI literacy testing. Dropped for overlap with prescribed challenges or weaker policy anchors.

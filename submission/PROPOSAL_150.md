Hong Kong's 2026 Policy Address (para 385) promises delivery platform workers statutory compensation for injuries at work, and the July 2026 proposal makes platforms liable, splitting liability when a rider works for several apps. Nothing records the crash. A 2024 union survey found only 13% of injured riders compensated; one typhoon case took five years in court.

Metrics is the work-injury event record. A coin-sized helmet sensor and the rider's phone detect a crash with a gradient-boosting model trained on public fall data (95% recall, 93% precision, leave-subjects-out) and our drop tests. Thirty seconds of silence sends location and a probable trauma profile to dispatch, and the verified event triggers a fixed parametric payout within a day. The record names the app active at impact, so apportionment becomes a lookup.

Riders own the raw data. Platforms and insurers see events, never routes or scores. Hong Kong writes the law. We write the standard.

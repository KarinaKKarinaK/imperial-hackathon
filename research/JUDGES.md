# Judges and what they will look for

Source: Imperial judges page (fetched 19 Oct 2026) and the 15 Oct 2026 online briefing deck (in repo root). The earlier judge list in CONTEXT.md named Lucas Cheung; the live panel has replaced him with Helen Kwok and added Dr Pamela Tin.

## The four judges

### Dr Jackie Bell, Imperial College London
Principal Teaching Fellow in Computing (the site says Senior Teaching Fellow). PhD in theoretical particle physics. Specialises in equity, diversity and inclusion in STEM, widening participation, informal science learning and science communication. Imperial President's Medal for Supporting the Student Experience 2024. Selected for the BBC's "Astronauts: Do You Have What It Takes?".

What she will probe: Is the solution designed for real people, including people who are not like us? Does it widen access or narrow it? Can every team member explain their part? Is the science communicated clearly to a non-specialist?

How we answer: riders are low-income, often migrant or older workers, 65% rely on the job as main income. Consent screen in plain language. The rider owns the data. Fatigue and heat alerts go to the rider only. Every member speaks in the video. The pipeline is explained in one picture.

### Professor Shahid Khan, Imperial College London and Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust
Professor of Practice (Hepatology), Director of Admissions, NHS Consultant Hepatologist and Gastroenterologist. Over 100 peer-reviewed publications, national clinical guidelines, chairs Cholangiocarcinoma UK. Research interests: epidemiology, biomarker development.

What he will probe: Is the evidence base real? Are the injury thresholds from peer-reviewed sources? What does the validation prove and not prove? Is the triage output clinically meaningful? Are we overselling?

How we answer: ten plus peer-reviewed sources in a structured table. Head impact thresholds cited to biomechanics literature. Drop tests reported with confidence intervals and an explicit statement that a flat drop is not a collision. Triage profile is labelled "probable", with mechanism and magnitude, not a diagnosis. Limits section on the poster and the site.

### Dr Pamela Tin, Chief Executive's Policy Unit, HKSAR Government
Research Director at CEPU, leads evidence-based research to inform policy. PhD in public health. Previously led health policy research at a Hong Kong think tank. Served on the Advisory Committee on Mental Health. Interests: primary healthcare, health workforce planning, health resource allocation, mental health.

What she will probe: Is the policy anchor exact? Does the solution help the government implement para 385, or does it get in the way? Is it feasible inside Hong Kong's institutions (Labour Department, Insurance Authority, PDPO)? What is the social return? Is the data governance credible?

How we answer: para 385 quoted verbatim. The Jul 2026 government proposal read and referenced, especially multi-platform apportionment, which our record solves directly. Social return framed as faster payouts, fewer contested claims, objective records for fatal cases, and a loss history the Labour Department does not have today. Governance mapped to PDPO principles. Note her mental health interest: the fatigue signal is framed as a worker-protective alert, never a score the platform sees.

### Helen Kwok, Google Hong Kong
Digital Strategy Lead. Digital advertising and cross-border business strategy. Previously regional advertising sales at Xiaohongshu (Rednote) and enterprise technology at AWS. Expertise: user acquisition, go-to-market, cross-border commercial strategy, enterprise cloud.

What she will probe: Who is the customer? Who pays? How do you acquire the first platform and the first insurer? Why does this cross the border? What stops a platform building it in-house?

How we answer: customers are platforms (liability holders under the new law) and insurers (pricing and claims). Riders never pay. Go-to-market: Insurance Authority Insurtech Sandbox pilot with one licensed insurer and one platform in Hong Kong, then the standard travels through insurers and reinsurers to the mainland scheme (84 million workers, 14 platforms) and Singapore (mandatory since Jan 2025). Defensibility: neutrality. A platform cannot be the trusted record of its own liability. The cross-border story is her home ground.

## What the judges said they want (briefing deck, 15 Oct 2026)

Looking for:
- Teams that understand the problem as well as the solution
- Vision presented clearly within time limits
- Honesty about limitations, no overselling
- Every member able to speak to their contribution
- Solutions that could realistically exist in Hong Kong or Macau
- Direct answers to questions

Not impressed by:
- Technically impressive solutions that do not address a real need
- Polished pitches with no substance
- Solutions that ignore the human or ethical dimension
- Teams where only one person can answer

What makes a team stand out:
- Design for real people, accessible and inclusive
- Practical, long-term impact that can be adopted and scaled
- Something tangible: prototype, demo, proof of concept
- Learn and adapt to feedback
- Ambition balanced with a realistic plan
- Genuine enthusiasm

Great pitch:
- Start with the human story
- Show scale, urgency, consequences
- Real scenarios and user journeys
- Evidence behind every claim
- Show the solution in action
- Clear and concise, every minute earns its place
- End with a vision

## Submission facts (FAQ page, fetched 19 Oct 2026)
- Written proposal: 150 words
- Video pitch: 3 to 5 minutes, English
- Deadline: 25 Oct 2026, 23:59 HKT. No late entries.
- Judging criteria named on the FAQ: creativity, innovation, feasibility, potential impact, interdisciplinarity, teamwork
- Grand Final: Saturday 14 Nov 2026, Data Technology Hub, Tseung Kwan O InnoPark, HKSTP. In person only. Slides emailed 48 hours before.
- Seven teams reach the Grand Final. Every finalist gets a podium or specialist award.

## Awards and how we target them
- Gold: scientific rigour, cross-disciplinary integration, credible pathway to impact, communication. HK$15,000, trophy, Imperial letter of recommendation.
- Specialist awards: best translational research, best innovation, greatest social impact, best STEMB integration, best sustainability.
- We are a natural fit for translational research (sandbox pathway), social impact (uninsured workers), STEMB integration (four pillars, each with a real deliverable) and innovation (apportionment record, parametric trigger, rider-owned data). We are not a sustainability entry and should not pretend to be.

## How this shapes the website
- Lead with numbers and the human story, not the technology.
- One section per judge lens without naming judges: Health and evidence. Inclusion and consent. Policy and feasibility. Market and go-to-market.
- A visible limits section.
- A live demo that any judge can run on their own phone.
- A STEMB map that shows what each pillar actually produced.
- A "built for the brief" checklist mapping to the five Imperial steps.
